# Loom Build: the look (2026-11-06)

Chris wants the Build view to feel sleek and fun to use and play with: Apple, liquid glass, apps
people enjoy. Fabcheck's board view is one reference but not the only one. This file records the
references, what Loom takes from each, and what it leaves alone.

## References

| Reference | What it does well | What Loom takes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Apple Liquid Glass** (iOS/macOS 26, WWDC25 sessions 219 and 356, *Adopting Liquid Glass*) | Glass is its own layer for controls and navigation, floating over the content. It is used sparingly, never glass on glass. Related controls share one capsule, and corners are concentric with their container. Controls respond to touch, and buttons morph into menus. It respects Reduce Transparency and Reduce Motion. | Glass capsules for the canvas controls only: panel toggles, Fit and Auto layout, Grid, zoom, and the wiring tip. Controls are grouped by function, with a specular top edge and a press that squishes. Nothing glass sits on other glass. The motion and transparency fallbacks are honored. |
| **Figma UI3** (*Our approach to designing UI3*) | Toolbars float over the canvas. Floating side panels were tried and then reverted: they cramped the canvas on smaller screens, and heavy users found them slower. "Speed is a feature." | The side panels stay docked and foldable. Only small controls float. Nothing slows a click down: no animation blocks input. |
| **Fabcheck board view** (this suite) | A deep near-black stage with the subject lit in the middle. Physical rendering (copper, mask). Glow marks what matters. Glass HUD segments. | The dark stage with a soft pool of light behind the diagram. The wire selection glows like a Fabcheck finding. The suite's accent stays the selection color. |
| **Blender node editor** (colored noodles, 3.0) and **Unreal Blueprints** with the Electronic Nodes plugin | Wires are the hero: thick, colored, smooth curves. Moving bubbles along a wire show flow, and the plugin limits them to exec wires. | Wires drawn as real insulation: a darker edge, a specular highlight down the middle and a soft shadow under them. A pulse of light travels along a wire only while it is selected. |
| **n8n, ComfyUI, React Flow** | Animated edges are used only for what is active or running, so the canvas stays calm. Ports are clear handles. | Animate only the selection and the wire being drawn. Each wire end gets a metal contact dot where it lands on a pin, a clear port. |
| **Origami Studio, Quartz Composer, Max/MSP** | Patching is the fun part: a rubber-band cable follows the cursor and snaps onto a port. | The wire being drawn is an elastic curve in the wire's color that follows the cursor. Free pins on other ends glow as targets. A new wire draws itself in from end to end. |
| **Apple Freeform, iOS drag and drop** | A picked-up object lifts: it grows a little and its shadow deepens, and it settles when dropped. The camera glides. | A dragged part lifts (scale and shadow). Fit and zoom glide with a spring ease rather than jumping. |
| **Apple Home, product renders, Teenage Engineering** | Parts read as objects: rounded bodies, soft light from above, crisp labels, color used only where it means something. | On the canvas, connectors and box labels are rounded cards with a soft drop shadow and a lit top edge, without spreadsheet grid lines. Wire colors stay the only saturated color. |
| **Linear, Raycast, Vercel** | Restraint: one accent, quiet hairlines, tight type, motion around 150–250 ms. | Hairline row separators, tabular numbers, and short, consistent timing (`--ease` and a spring curve). |
| **WireViz, RapidHarness, Wirely** (harness tools) | The table-and-wire diagram techs already read. | Unchanged on the drawing sheet. The sheet keeps the WireViz look and prints in black and white. Only the canvas gets the new look (`opts.fx`). |

## Rules Loom follows

1. **Content first, glass second.** The diagram is the content. Glass is only on the small floating
   controls, never on the docked panels and never stacked.
2. **Same drawing, two finishes.** `conn.js` draws one diagram. With `opts.fx` (the canvas) it adds
   the finish: card corners, wire gloss and shadow, contact dots. Without it (the sheet) the output
   is unchanged.
3. **Color means something.** Wire colors are real insulation colors. The accent marks selection
   and the wire being drawn. Everything else is neutral.
4. **Animate what is in focus.** At rest nothing moves. A selection glows and pulses, a wire being
   drawn stretches, a new wire draws in, a dragged part lifts and the camera glides.
5. **Never in the way.** No animation delays input. Re-rendering mid-animation doesn't restart the
   pulse, which is phase-locked to the clock. Reduce Motion turns the motion off, and Reduce
   Transparency turns off the glass blur.
